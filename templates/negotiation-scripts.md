# Negotiation Scripts — Word for Word

> Practice these out loud before your first offer call. Fluency under pressure requires rehearsal.

---

## Before the Offer — Deflecting Salary Questions

**When asked "What are your salary expectations?" early in process:**

> "I'd like to understand the full scope and requirements of the role before naming a number. What's the budgeted range for this position?"

**If they push:**

> "I'm genuinely flexible — I care more about fit and total compensation than a specific base number. That said, based on my research into the market for this role, I'd expect to be in the range of $[floor]–$[anchor]. But I'm open to discussing the full package."

**If they absolutely insist on a number:**

> "Based on my research, I'm targeting $[P90 of market]. That said, I'd want to see the full offer before committing to anything."

---

## Receiving the Offer — Never Accept on the Call

**When the offer comes verbally:**

> "I'm really excited about this — thank you. I'd like a day to review the full offer letter before responding. Can you send it over and I'll come back to you by [specific day]?"

**Why this matters:** This buys time to (a) research comps, (b) accelerate competing processes, and (c) consult advisors. Accepting on the call is almost always a mistake.

---

## Countering the Offer

**Countering base salary:**

> "I'm genuinely excited to join the team. Based on my research into the market rate for this role and the scope of the position, I was expecting something closer to $[P90]. Is there flexibility to get there?"

*Then stop talking. Silence is the most powerful negotiation tactic.*

**If they ask why:**

> "It's based on market research across [Levels.fyi / Glassdoor / LinkedIn Salary] for comparable roles at companies at your stage. I want to make sure we start in the right place."

---

## When They Say "This Is Our Best Offer"

> "I really appreciate you going to bat for me — I know that's not always easy. Is there any flexibility on the signing bonus or the equity component to bridge the gap? Even a one-time amount would make this very easy to say yes to today."

**Follow-up if they still hold:**

> "I understand. Let me think about whether there are other components of the package we could discuss — I want to make this work."

---

## Using a Competing Offer as Leverage

> "I want to be transparent with you because I'd genuinely prefer to be at [Company]. I have another offer on the table at $[X]. If you can get closer to that number, I can commit by [specific date]. Is that something you can work with?"

**Rules:**
- Only use if you actually have another offer
- Name the competing number — vagueness weakens your leverage
- Give them a deadline — creates urgency and shows you're serious

---

## Negotiating Specific Package Components

**Signing bonus:**

> "Is there room on a signing bonus? I'd be walking away from unvested equity at my current company, so a one-time payment would help me make this transition."

**Equity:**

> "Can we look at increasing the equity component? I'm excited about [Company]'s trajectory and I'd love to have more skin in the game."

**Remote flexibility:**

> "One thing that matters to me is flexibility on remote work. Is it possible to build in [X] remote days per week as part of the arrangement?"

**Title:**

> "The role scope sounds like a [Senior/Lead/Principal] level. Is there any flexibility on the title to reflect that?"

**Early performance review:**

> "I understand the base salary has limits. Would you be open to building in a compensation review at the 6-month mark, tied to specific milestones? That would help me feel confident about the growth trajectory."

---

## Asking for More Time

**To evaluate the offer:**

> "I want to make sure I'm making the right long-term decision. Could I have until [date — 5–7 days out] to finalize? I'm very interested — I just want to be thoughtful about something this significant."

**If they pressure for a quick decision:**

> "I want to say yes and I want to mean it. Giving me [X days] makes sure that's the case. Is [date] workable?"

---

## Accepting the Offer

> "I'm excited to accept. Before I give notice at my current company, can you confirm the key terms in writing — [base, bonus %, equity, start date, and [any negotiated terms]]? Once I have that, I'll move quickly."

**Always get the offer in writing before resigning from your current job.**

---

## Declining an Offer Gracefully

> "Thank you so much for the offer and the time you invested in me through the process. After careful consideration, I've decided to pursue a different opportunity that's a closer fit for where I want to go. I have enormous respect for what [Company] is building and I hope our paths cross again."

*Keep it brief, positive, and final. Don't explain at length or invite a counter.*

---

## Pre-Negotiation Prep Checklist

Before any offer call:

- [ ] Market research done (3+ sources, know P50/P75/P90)
- [ ] Walk-away number set (the minimum I'd accept)
- [ ] Target number set (P75 — what I'm aiming for)
- [ ] Anchor number set (P90 — what I'll name first)
- [ ] Full package priorities ranked (base / equity / bonus / remote / title)
- [ ] Competing offers or timelines I can reference
- [ ] Scripts practiced out loud at least twice
- [ ] Silence practice: can I hold for 10+ seconds without filling it?
