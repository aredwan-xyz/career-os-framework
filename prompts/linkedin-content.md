# AI Prompts — LinkedIn Content

> Use these to write all your LinkedIn posts. Aim for 3 per week.
> Replace everything in `[brackets]` before using.

---

## Post Type 1: Insight / Lesson Learned

```
Write a LinkedIn post sharing a professional insight or lesson.

My role/niche: [e.g. "ML engineer at AI startups"]
The insight: [describe what you want to share — be specific]
Evidence or story behind it: [brief context — what happened that taught you this]
Target audience: [who reads your LinkedIn — recruiters, peers, founders, etc.]

Requirements:
- Hook in line 1 — make it impossible to scroll past
- No "I'm excited to share" or corporate preamble
- Short paragraphs (2–3 sentences max each)
- First-person, conversational voice — no buzzwords
- 150–250 words
- End with a genuine question to start discussion
- Do NOT include more than 3 hashtags (if any)

Hook formula options to try:
- "Most [practitioners] do X. We tried Y. Here's what actually happened."
- "I've been doing [X] wrong for [N] years. Here's what changed."
- "Unpopular opinion: [your contrarian take]"
- "[Number] things I wish someone told me about [topic]:"
```

## Post Type 2: Proof / Results Post

```
Write a LinkedIn post sharing a professional result or achievement.

The result: [metric — be specific: %, $, time saved, users, etc.]
Context (the problem): [what the challenge was before you addressed it]
What I did: [key approach in 1–2 sentences — not every detail]
Unexpected insight or lesson: [something surprising or counterintuitive from the process]
What others can apply: [practical takeaway for the reader]

Requirements:
- LEAD with the number in line 1 (the result IS the hook)
- Structure: result → problem → approach → outcome → lesson
- Include 1–2 specific decisions that made the difference
- Authentic — not a humble brag. The insight is the value, not just the number.
- 200–300 words
- End with: "What's your experience with [related challenge]?"
- Screenshots, charts, or before/after data get 3x the reach — suggest where I could add one
```

## Post Type 3: Opinion / Hot Take

```
Write a LinkedIn opinion post arguing a clear position.

My position: [your specific take — must be specific and defensible]
The common belief I'm pushing back on: [what most people in my field assume]
Why I believe my take is right: [2–3 reasons or pieces of evidence]
The strongest counterargument: [steel-man the other side]
My response to that counterargument: [why you still hold your position]

Requirements:
- Line 1 = the bold take. No preamble. No "I've been thinking about..."
- Pick a clear side — no "both sides have merit" fence-sitting
- Acknowledge the counterargument (shows intellectual honesty)
- Defend your position in the final paragraph
- 150–250 words
- Tone: confident, a bit provocative, but not mean or dismissive
- The post should make at least some readers want to disagree in the comments — that's the point
- End with a direct question inviting disagreement: "What do you think? Am I wrong?"
```

## Post Type 4: Career Story / Journey Post

```
Write a LinkedIn post sharing a turning point in my career.

The moment or decision: [describe it specifically]
What I thought at the time: [your mindset then]
What actually happened: [the outcome, with specifics]
What it taught me: [the insight]
Why I'm sharing it now: [why this is relevant to your audience today]

Requirements:
- Personal and honest — not a polished success story
- Include the moment of doubt or difficulty (relatability beats perfection)
- Specific details over general claims
- 200–300 words
- Tone: reflective, warm, first-person
- End with what you'd tell someone facing the same decision today
```

## Post Type 5: Industry Observation

```
Write a LinkedIn post about a trend or shift I'm observing in [my industry/field].

The observation: [what you're noticing — be specific about what's changing]
Evidence I've seen: [2–3 concrete data points, examples, or conversations]
What this means for [practitioners/companies/hiring]: [your interpretation]
What people should do about it: [practical implication]

Requirements:
- Frame as an observation, not a prediction (more credible, less backlash)
- Include at least 2 concrete examples or data points — not just assertions
- 200–300 words
- Invites discussion without being preachy
- End with: "Are you seeing this too? What am I missing?"
```

## Post Editing Prompt

```
Edit this LinkedIn post to make it stronger.

[paste draft post]

Specific issues to fix:
- [e.g. "hook isn't strong enough"]
- [e.g. "too long"]
- [e.g. "sounds corporate"]

Requirements:
- Keep my voice — don't make it sound like a different person
- Hook must be the first line and must stop the scroll
- Cut any sentence that isn't earning its place
- No buzzwords, no passive voice, no corporate speak
- Return the edited version + explain the top 3 changes you made and why
```

## Content Calendar Planning Prompt

```
I'm a [role] in [industry/niche]. Help me plan 4 weeks of LinkedIn content.

My audience: [who follows/should follow me — recruiters, peers, founders, etc.]
My goals: [e.g. "attract senior PM roles at Series B startups"]
My recent professional activities: [what you've been working on lately]
My strongest opinions about my field: [2–3 things you believe strongly]

Create a 4-week calendar (3 posts/week = 12 posts total) with:
- Post type for each (Insight / Proof / Opinion / Story / Observation)
- Topic or angle for each post
- Estimated reach potential for each (Low/Medium/High) based on the topic
- Which posts to prioritize if I can only do 1–2 per week

Vary the types — don't cluster all opinions or all proof posts together.
```
