# AI Prompts — Thank You Emails & Post-Interview Follow-Up

---

## Core Thank You Email

```
Write a post-interview thank you email.

Interviewer name and title: [Name, Title]
Company: [Company]
Role I interviewed for: [Role Title]
Interview format: [phone screen / video / onsite / panel]
Date: [today's date]

Specific moment from our conversation I want to reference:
[a question they asked, something they said about the team/company, 
a challenge they described, a topic we went deep on — be specific]

One new value-add or insight I want to include:
[an idea relevant to their problem, a relevant article, a thought that 
occurred to me after the interview, something I forgot to mention]

Requirements:
- Subject line: "Thank you — [Role] interview" (or suggest a more creative variant)
- Open by referencing the specific conversation moment (not a generic "great to meet you")
- 2–3 sentences on why I'm more interested after the conversation
- Include the value-add naturally — not forced
- Close confidently — not "I hope to hear from you"
- Under 120 words total
- Tone: professional warmth, not gushing
- Do not use: "I just wanted to", "circling back", "per my last email"
```

---

## Thank You Email — Multiple Interviewers (Panel)

```
I had a panel interview today with [N] people. Write [N] separate thank you emails — 
one for each person. Each email must be different.

Interviewers:
1. [Name, Title] — [one thing they specifically said or asked]
2. [Name, Title] — [one thing they specifically said or asked]  
3. [Name, Title] — [one thing they specifically said or asked]

Role: [Role Title] at [Company]

For each email:
- Reference something specific that person said or asked
- Angle the value-add toward their specific role/perspective
- Under 100 words each
- Each one must feel personal and distinct — not variations of the same template
```

## Follow-Up After No Response — Day 5

```
Write a brief follow-up email for [Role] at [Company].

It has been 5 days since my interview with [Name] and I haven't heard back.

Requirements:
- Under 50 words
- Polite and confident — not anxious or apologetic
- Restate interest in one sentence
- Give them an easy action (reply or call)
- Do not re-pitch or re-summarize qualifications
```

---

## Follow-Up After Rejection — Staying on Their Radar

```
Write a gracious reply to a rejection email for [Role] at [Company].

The rejection was: [form email / personalized note / phone call]
Reason given (if any): [e.g. "went with someone with more enterprise experience"]

Requirements:
- Express genuine thanks for the process
- Ask for feedback (briefly — one sentence)
- Express continued interest in the company for future roles
- Leave a positive final impression — this person may be your contact in 6 months
- Under 80 words
- No bitterness, no "I understand but..." rebuttals
```

---

## Asking for Feedback After Rejection

```
Write an email asking for feedback after being rejected for [Role] at [Company].

Rejected by: [Name, Title]
Stage I reached: [phone screen / first round / final round / offer rescinded]
Tone I want: [curious and grateful, not defensive]

Requirements:
- Thank them for their time and the process first
- Make the feedback ask specific — not "any feedback?" but "where I fell short" or 
  "what would have made me a stronger candidate"
- Acknowledge they may not have time to respond
- Under 80 words
- This should feel like a learner asking for coaching, not a candidate challenging a decision
```
