# AI Prompts — Outreach & LinkedIn Content

---

## Outreach Prompts

### Hiring Manager DM (Post-Application)

```
Write a LinkedIn DM to send to a hiring manager after applying for their role.

Their name: [Name]
Their role: [Title at Company]
Role I applied for: [Job Title]
My most relevant result: [specific metric or achievement]
Tone: confident, direct, not desperate

Requirements:
- Under 75 words
- Don't ask for a job — just make a connection
- Reference a specific result in 1 sentence
- End with a low-pressure close ("either way, best of luck with the search")
- Don't start with "I hope this message finds you well"
```

---

### Informational Interview Request

```
Write a LinkedIn DM requesting an informational interview.

Their name: [Name]
Their role: [Title at Company]
Why I'm reaching out to THEM specifically: [genuine reason — their background, their company's work, something they wrote]
My background in 1 sentence: [role + niche]
What I want to learn: [specific topic or question]

Requirements:
- Under 60 words
- Ask for 15–20 minutes
- Make it easy to say yes — suggest Calendly or "happy to work around your schedule"
- Specific reason for reaching out to this person (not generic)
- Warm but professional tone
```

---

### Cold Outreach (No Open Role)

```
Write a cold LinkedIn DM for someone at a company I want to work at, with no open role.

Their name: [Name]
Their role: [Title]
Company: [Company]
Something specific I know about their work: [detail from research]
My value proposition: [what I do / what I could bring]

Requirements:
- Under 80 words
- Lead with what I know about them / their company (not about me)
- One line on who I am and what I do
- A specific, low-friction ask
- Not "I'd love to join your team" — frame as curiosity about their work
```

---

### Referral Request (Weak Connection)

```
Write a message asking for a referral or intro from someone I know casually.

Their name: [Name]
How we know each other: [context]
Company I'm targeting: [Company]
Role I'm targeting: [Role]
Why I'd be a good fit in 1 sentence: [key credential]

Requirements:
- Warm and personal — not copy-paste corporate
- Acknowledge it's an ask (don't pretend it's not)
- Give them an easy out ("totally fine either way")
- Under 80 words
- Make it easy for them to forward — include a 1-sentence summary of who I am
```

---

### Follow-Up (Day 5 — No Response)

```
Write a brief follow-up message for an application with no response after 5 days.

Original application: [Role at Company]
Contact: [Name, if known]

Requirements:
- Under 40 words
- No re-pitching — just staying visible
- Polite and confident
- One sentence on still being interested
- One sentence inviting them to reach out
```

## LinkedIn Content Prompts

### Insight Post

```
Write a LinkedIn post in my voice.

My role: [title/niche]
The insight or lesson: [describe what you want to share]
Evidence or story behind it: [brief context]

Requirements:
- Hook on line 1 — make it impossible to scroll past
- No corporate speak or buzzwords
- First person, direct, conversational
- 150–250 words max
- End with a question to drive comments
- Do NOT include: "Excited to share", "Thrilled to announce", emoji clusters, or #10hashtags
- Write in short paragraphs (1–3 sentences each) — no walls of text
```

---

### Proof / Result Post

```
Write a LinkedIn post sharing a professional result.

What I achieved: [metric / outcome]
Context: [what the challenge was]
How I did it: [key approach in 1–2 sentences]
What others can learn from it: [takeaway]

Requirements:
- Lead with the number in line 1 (the result is the hook)
- Structure: result → problem → approach → outcome → lesson
- Include 1–2 specific tactics or decisions that made the difference
- 200–300 words
- End with a question or invitation to share their experience
- Authentic — not a humble brag
```

---

### Opinion / Hot Take Post

```
Write a LinkedIn opinion post arguing a specific position.

My position: [your take — be specific and bold]
The common belief I'm challenging: [what most people think]
My evidence or reasoning: [why you believe your take]

Requirements:
- Line 1 must be the bold take — no preamble
- Take a clear side — no "on one hand... on the other hand"
- Acknowledge the counterargument in 1–2 sentences
- End by defending your position
- 150–250 words
- Tone: confident, a bit provocative, not aggressive
- This should make some people disagree — that's the point
```

### Profile Recommendation Ask

```
Write a message asking a former colleague or manager for a LinkedIn recommendation.

Their name: [Name]
My relationship to them: [e.g. "you were my manager for 2 years at Acme"]
What I'd like them to focus on: [specific project/skill/quality]
Context for why I'm asking now: [e.g. "I'm exploring new opportunities"]

Requirements:
- Personal and specific — not a template they can tell is generic
- Make it easy by suggesting the focus area
- Offer to reciprocate
- Under 100 words
```
