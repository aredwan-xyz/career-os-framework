# Module 01 — Identity Architecture

> **Timeline:** Week 1–2  
> **Goal:** Know precisely who you are, what you're worth, and who wants you most

Before a single application goes out, you need to define your position in the market. This is the foundation everything else is built on. Skip it and you'll spray-and-pray. Do it well and every downstream step becomes 10x easier.

---

## The Four Pillars

### 1. Deep Skill Audit

List every hard and soft skill you have. Be exhaustive — include things that feel obvious to you (those are often your biggest advantages, because they're invisible to you).

**Step-by-step:**

1. Open a blank doc. Set a 30-minute timer.
2. List every technical skill (tools, languages, frameworks, methodologies)
3. List every domain skill (industries, business functions, problems you've solved)
4. List every soft skill (communication, leadership, systems thinking, negotiation)
5. Rate each skill 1–10 on: **competence** and **enjoyment**
6. Identify your top 5 "unfair advantages" — things you do that most people genuinely can't

**Then run a gap analysis:**

- Pull 20 job descriptions from your target roles
- Copy all required/preferred skills into a spreadsheet
- Count frequency of each skill across all 20 JDs
- Cross-reference against your rated skills
- The high-frequency / low-score skills are your priority gaps to close

> **Use the AI prompt:** [`prompts/cv-tailoring.md#skill-gap`](../prompts/cv-tailoring.md)

### 2. Niche Definition

Your niche sits at the intersection of three circles:

```
          What you're
          great at
             /\
            /  \
           /    \
          /  ★   \
         /        \
What the ─────────── What you
market pays         can prove
premium for         with evidence
```

**The niche formula:**

```
I am the go-to [role] for [specific company type] 
who need to [specific outcome] 
using [your method/approach].
```

**Examples:**

| Generic (weak) | Niche (strong) |
|----------------|----------------|
| Product Manager | PM specializing in 0→1 B2B SaaS products at seed-stage startups |
| Data Scientist | ML engineer who reduces LLM inference costs for AI-native companies |
| Marketing Manager | Growth marketer for D2C health brands scaling from $1M–$10M ARR |
| UX Designer | Mobile-first UX designer for fintech apps targeting Gen Z users |

**How to validate your niche:**
- Search your niche on LinkedIn Jobs — are there 50+ roles in the last 90 days? ✅
- Are the roles paying at or above your salary target? ✅
- Are you genuinely qualified for 60%+ of the postings? ✅

If all three are yes, you have a viable niche. If not, adjust.

### 3. Ideal Employer Profile (IEP)

Define exactly what kind of employer you want before looking for one. This filter saves you from wasting time on roles that will make you miserable even if you land them.

**IEP Framework:**

| Dimension | Options | Your Choice |
|-----------|---------|-------------|
| Company stage | Pre-seed / Seed / Series A–B / Series C+ / Public / Enterprise | |
| Company size | 1–20 / 20–100 / 100–500 / 500–2000 / 2000+ | |
| Industry / sector | SaaS / Fintech / Healthcare / Climate / Consumer / etc. | |
| Work model | Remote / Hybrid (X days) / On-site | |
| Geography | City / Country / Timezone requirement | |
| Culture signals | Move fast / Process-oriented / Research-led / Mission-driven | |
| Team structure | Flat / Hierarchical / Matrix | |
| Revenue model | B2B SaaS / Marketplace / Enterprise / Consumer | |

**Build your target company list:**

1. Start with companies you already admire
2. Search LinkedIn for companies matching your IEP
3. Check Crunchbase/AngelList for companies in your stage/sector
4. Ask in your network for recommendations
5. Aim for 50–100 companies — this is your hunting ground

**Categorize into:**
- **Tier 1** (20 companies): Dream employers. Full effort here.
- **Tier 2** (30 companies): Strong fit. Active pursuit.
- **Tier 3** (50 companies): Acceptable fit. Apply opportunistically.

### 4. Salary Intelligence

Know your number before any conversation starts. Walking into a negotiation without market data is like negotiating a house price without knowing the neighborhood comps.

**Salary Research Stack:**
- [Levels.fyi](https://levels.fyi) — best for tech roles, very granular
- [Glassdoor](https://glassdoor.com) — broad coverage, self-reported
- [LinkedIn Salary](https://linkedin.com/salary) — requires Premium
- [Comprehensive.io](https://comprehensive.io) — startup equity data
- [Blind](https://teamblind.com) — anonymous forums, tech-focused
- [Payscale](https://payscale.com) — non-tech roles
- [H1B Salary Database](https://h1bdata.info) — actual filed salaries

**Set three numbers:**
- **Floor:** P50 of market — the minimum you'd accept
- **Target:** P75 — what you're aiming for
- **Anchor:** P90 — what you'll name first in negotiation

## Your Positioning Statement

One sentence. Repeat everywhere — LinkedIn, cover letters, intros at events, cold DMs.

**Template:**
```
I help [company type] achieve [specific outcome] 
by [your method/skill], so they can [ultimate benefit].
```

**Good examples:**
```
I help Series B fintech startups reduce fraud losses 40% 
by building real-time ML detection pipelines, 
so their risk teams can scale without adding headcount.
```

```
I help D2C health brands double their subscription retention
by redesigning onboarding UX using behavioral science,
so they stop losing customers in the first 30 days.
```

**Bad examples (too vague):**
```
I help companies build better software products. ❌
I am a data scientist with 5 years of experience. ❌
Passionate professional seeking new opportunities. ❌
```

## Common Mistakes

**Mistake 1: Defining your niche too broadly.**  
"I'm a software engineer" is not a niche. Go specific enough that a recruiter searching your niche would find fewer than 1,000 people. If you're describing yourself and it sounds like 100,000 LinkedIn profiles, narrow it.

**Mistake 2: Letting your current job title define your niche.**  
Your title is an artifact of your employer's org chart. Your niche should be defined by the problems you solve and outcomes you produce, not the box you sit in.

**Mistake 3: Skipping this module because "I already know what I want."**  
Everyone thinks they know. Almost no one has written it down in a form that's specific, validated against the market, and tied to evidence they can provide. Do the work.
