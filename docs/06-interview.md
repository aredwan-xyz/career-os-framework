# Module 06 — Interview Mastery

> **Timeline:** Rolling — start preparation immediately upon scheduling  
> **Goal:** Walk in prepared for every question. Walk out as the obvious choice.

---

## Pre-Interview Research Protocol (2h minimum)

**Company research:**
- [ ] Last 2 annual reports or investor letters (or funding announcements for startups)
- [ ] Recent press releases and news (last 90 days) — Google News + LinkedIn company page
- [ ] Glassdoor reviews — read the "Cons" section; it's the most honest
- [ ] All current job openings — they reveal priorities and gaps
- [ ] Their product or service — actually use it if possible
- [ ] Their competitors — how does this company differentiate?
- [ ] Recent executive LinkedIn posts — what are leaders thinking about?

**Interviewer research (per person):**
- [ ] LinkedIn: career trajectory, mutual connections, posts/articles they've written
- [ ] Google: any talks, podcasts, papers, or press mentions
- [ ] Note: where did they come from? What problems have they worked on?

**Role research:**
- [ ] What are the top 3 business outcomes this role drives?
- [ ] What does "excellent" look like vs. "adequate" in this role?
- [ ] What are the likely biggest challenges in the first 90 days?
- [ ] What tech stack, tools, or methods will you need?

## STAR Story Bank — 15 Stories

Build these before your first interview. Update them as you have new experiences.

### Category A: Impact Stories (5)
Revenue grown, costs saved, users retained, products shipped, systems built.

**Template:**
- **Situation:** Context in 1–2 sentences (company, team, challenge)
- **Task:** What you were responsible for
- **Action:** What you specifically did (use "I", not "we")
- **Result:** Quantified outcome + timeframe

**Required for every story:**
- A number (%, $, time, users, etc.)
- Your specific contribution (not the team's)
- A lesson or what you'd do differently

### Category B: Challenge Stories (5)
Failures, conflicts, ambiguity, constraint, mistakes.

These show self-awareness, resilience, and growth mindset. Most candidates avoid them. Interviewers love them because they reveal character.

**Types to cover:**
- A project that failed — what happened and what you learned
- A conflict with a colleague or manager — how you resolved it
- A time you had to navigate ambiguity without clear direction
- A decision you made with incomplete information
- A time you received critical feedback and what you did with it

### Category C: Leadership Stories (5)
Even if you don't manage people, you lead — through influence, decisions, and vision.

**Types to cover:**
- Influencing people without authority
- Making a decision when others disagreed
- Communicating a difficult message
- Mentoring or developing someone
- Changing direction after new information

## AI Mock Interview System

See [`prompts/mock-interview.md`](../prompts/mock-interview.md) for the full prompt.

**Quick prompt:**
```
You are a tough interviewer for: [role + company]

Ask me 6 questions:
- 2 behavioral (STAR format)
- 2 situational (hypothetical scenarios)
- 1 technical / domain knowledge question
- 1 curveball

After each of my answers, score it 1–10 and tell me exactly:
what was strong, what was weak, and one specific improvement.
Be harsh — I want to prepare for the most challenging interviewer.
```

Run this 3x before any important interview. Record yourself answering. Watch once.

## Interview Day Protocol

### 24h Before
- Send a confirmation email + one smart, specific question about the role
- Re-read your STAR stories — don't cram, just refresh
- Lay out your clothes, confirm the link or address, charge your devices
- Stop prepping 2h before the interview — trust the work you've done

### Opening Move — The Unsolicited Pitch
Before they ask the first question:

```
"Before we dive in — I've done some preparation on [specific challenge 
from their JD or recent news]. I'd love to share a 2-minute take on 
how I'd approach it, if that's useful to you."
```

This instantly signals: you've done your homework, you take initiative, and you come with value rather than just credentials. Most candidates wait to be asked. Don't.

### Answering Questions
- Pause 3–5 seconds before answering tough questions — it signals thoughtfulness
- Use STAR structure for behavioral questions
- Quantify every answer where possible
- When you don't know something: "I haven't worked with that specifically, but here's how I'd approach learning it quickly..."
- Never speak negatively about past employers

### Closing Strong
**Always ask this question before you leave:**

> "Is there anything in this conversation that would make you hesitant to move me forward?"

This surfaces objections while you're still in the room. 90% of candidates never ask it. The 10% who do get to address concerns in real time instead of losing the role to a silent doubt the interviewer never raised.

Then ask: "What are the next steps and your timeline?"

### Post-Interview
**Within 2 hours:** Send a personalized thank-you email.

- Reference one specific moment from the conversation
- Add one new insight you'd bring to the role
- Keep it under 120 words
- Send to every interviewer individually (different content for each)

**Day 5:** Follow up if you haven't heard anything.  
**Day 10:** One final follow-up. After this, move on emotionally but stay technically in the pipeline.
