# Module 07 — Offer Negotiation

> **Timeline:** Offer stage  
> **Goal:** Never accept the first number. One 10-minute conversation changes your entire career trajectory.

---

## The Negotiation Mindset

**Three truths that most people don't act on:**
1. Every offer has negotiation room. "This is our best offer" is rarely true.
2. A polite counter never loses you an offer. The worst they can say is no.
3. Negotiating $10,000 more in base salary is worth $500,000+ over 20 years (raises and future offers are anchored to your current salary).

---

## Full Playbook

### Rule 1: Never give a number first

When asked for salary expectations before an offer:

```
"I'd like to understand the full scope and responsibility of the role 
before naming a number. What's the budgeted range for this position?"
```

If they push: "I'm flexible — I'd like to evaluate the full package and fit first."

If they insist: "Based on my research, roles like this at companies at your stage typically range from $X–$Y. I'd expect to be in that range, but I'm open to discussing the full package."

---

### Rule 2: Never accept on the call

When you receive the offer verbally:

```
"I'm really excited about this — thank you. I'd like a day to review 
the full offer letter before responding. Can you send it over and I'll 
come back to you by [specific day/time]?"
```

This buys you time to: (a) research the market, (b) consult advisors, (c) see if other processes can be accelerated.

---

### Rule 3: Counter at P90

Research P75–P90 of market rate for your role, level, and location. Name that number.

```
"I'm genuinely excited to join the team. Based on my research into 
the market rate for this role and the scope of the position, I was 
expecting something closer to $[X]. Is there flexibility to get there?"
```

Do not explain at length. Do not apologize. State the number and pause — silence is your friend here.

---

### Rule 4: Negotiate the whole package

When base salary is stuck, move to other levers:

| Lever | What to say |
|-------|-------------|
| Signing bonus | "Is there room on a signing bonus to bridge the gap?" |
| Equity | "Can we increase the equity component?" |
| Remote days | "Can we build in [X] remote days per week?" |
| Start date | "I'd need until [date] to transition properly" |
| Title | "Would a [Senior/Lead/Principal] title be on the table?" |
| 6-month review | "Can we agree to a compensation review at 6 months?" |
| Vacation | "What's the flexibility on PTO?" |
| Professional development budget | "Is there a learning/conference budget?" |

## Word-for-Word Scripts

See [`templates/negotiation-scripts.md`](../templates/negotiation-scripts.md) for the full library.

**Five most important scripts:**

```
1. COUNTERING BASE SALARY
"I'm genuinely excited to join. Based on my research, I was expecting 
closer to $[X]. Is there flexibility to get there?"

2. WHEN THEY SAY "THIS IS OUR BEST OFFER"
"I appreciate you going to bat for me. Is there any flexibility on 
the signing bonus or equity to bridge the gap? Even a one-time amount 
would make this easy to say yes to today."

3. USING A COMPETING OFFER
"I want to be transparent — I have another offer at $[X]. I'd 
genuinely prefer to be at [Company] because [specific reason]. 
If you can get closer, I can commit by Friday."

4. ASKING FOR MORE TIME
"I want to make sure I'm making the right decision. Could I have 
until [date 5–7 days out] to finalize? I'm very interested — I just 
want to be thoughtful."

5. ACCEPTING
"I'm excited to accept. Can you confirm [key terms] in writing 
so I can give notice at my current company?"
```
