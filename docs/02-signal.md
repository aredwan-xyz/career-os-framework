# Module 02 — Signal Building

> **Timeline:** Week 2–4, then ongoing permanently  
> **Goal:** Make the right people find you before you find them

Signal building is a background process that runs in parallel with everything else. The goal is to become unmissable to recruiters and hiring managers in your niche — so that by the time you apply, they've already seen your name.

---

## LinkedIn Overhaul — Complete 12-Point Checklist

LinkedIn is your professional storefront. Most people leave 80% of its value on the table with a thin, generic profile.

### Profile Sections

**Headline** (most important field after your name)
- Format: `[Role] | [Value Prop] | [1–2 keywords]`
- Bad: `Senior Software Engineer at Acme Corp`
- Good: `ML Engineer | Reducing inference costs for AI startups | Python · LLMs · MLOps`

**About Section** (the only place for your voice)
- Open with a hook — a result or observation, not "I am a..."
- 3–4 short paragraphs: who you help → what you do → evidence → CTA
- End with: "If you're building [X], let's talk. [email or 'DM me here']"
- 300–500 words maximum. Most people won't read beyond that.

**Experience Bullets**
- Every role needs 3–5 bullets
- Formula per bullet: `[Action verb] + [what you did] + [measurable result]`
- Examples:
  - ✅ `Reduced model inference latency 62% by implementing ONNX quantization, saving $40K/month in GPU costs`
  - ❌ `Responsible for machine learning model optimization and deployment`

**Skills Section**
- Add 20+ skills — these are search keywords
- Prioritize skills that appear frequently in your target JDs
- Get colleagues to endorse your top 5–8 skills

**Featured Section**
- Best 2–3 pieces of proof: GitHub repo, case study PDF, article, portfolio site
- These appear at the top of your profile — make them count

**Recommendations**
- Aim for 3+ from direct managers, clients, or senior colleagues
- Ask specifically: "Could you write something focused on [specific project/skill]?"
- Offer to reciprocate

### Settings
- [ ] Custom URL: `linkedin.com/in/firstname-lastname`
- [ ] "Open to Work" set to Recruiters only (if currently employed)
- [ ] Creator mode ON (unlocks more reach for posts)
- [ ] 500+ connections (credibility threshold — LinkedIn surfaces you more in search)

## The Content Engine — 3 Posts Per Week

LinkedIn's algorithm rewards consistency. 3 posts per week for 8 weeks equals 24 posts and measurable profile view increases. Most candidates post 0 times. This alone separates you from 90% of the field.

### The Three Post Types

**Type 1: Insight Post** (Mon/Wed)
- A contrarian take or hard-earned lesson from your work
- Structure: Hook line → Context (2–3 sentences) → The insight → What it means
- Length: 150–250 words
- Hook formulas that work:
  - "Most [practitioners] do X. We tried Y. Here's what happened."
  - "Unpopular opinion: [contrarian view in your field]"
  - "I made a mistake 2 years ago that cost [company/me] $X. Here's what I learned."

**Type 2: Proof Post** (Tue/Thu)
- Show a result, project, or before/after transformation
- Lead with the metric, explain how you got there
- Structure: Number/result → Problem → Approach → Outcome → Takeaway
- Include screenshots, charts, or code snippets when possible — they earn 3x reach

**Type 3: Opinion Post** (Fri)
- Take a clear, specific position on an industry debate
- Polarizing = engagement = algorithm amplification
- Not "here are both sides" — that earns nothing. Pick a side.
- Examples:
  - "RAG is overused and most teams shouldn't be building it. Here's why."
  - "The 'we hire for culture fit' excuse is often code for bias. Fight me."
  - "The best UX I've seen this year is from a company no one talks about."

### Content Scheduling Tips
- Write all 3 posts in one 45-minute session per week
- Schedule Mon/Wed/Fri using LinkedIn's native scheduler or Buffer
- Respond to every comment within 2 hours of posting — engagement in hour 1 is algorithmic fuel
- Never delete a post. Even low-performing posts build consistency signals.

## Portfolio / Proof Layer

Your portfolio is the evidence behind your positioning. It should be a single URL that proves everything you claim.

### For Technical Roles

**GitHub profile:**
- Pin 3–4 most impressive repos
- Every pinned repo needs: a clear README with problem/solution/impact, a demo GIF or screenshots, and deployment instructions
- Contribution graph should show consistent activity — if it's empty, start committing

**Case studies:**
- 2–3 written case studies, hosted on Notion or a simple site
- Structure: Problem → Constraints → Approach → Implementation → Results → Lessons
- Include real numbers wherever possible (even ranges if exact numbers are confidential)

### For Non-Technical Roles

**Work samples:**
- Curated PDF portfolio of 3–5 best deliverables (redact confidential info)
- Each sample needs a 1-paragraph context explanation: what was the goal, what was your role, what was the outcome

**Thought leadership:**
- Substack or Medium for longer-form writing
- Even 4–5 well-written posts establishes credibility
- LinkedIn articles for shorter format

**The Brag Doc:**
- A private 2-page document listing every significant win in each role
- Quantified: revenue generated/influenced, costs saved, users retained, projects shipped, team members managed
- This feeds your CV, cover letters, STAR stories, and LinkedIn posts
- Update it monthly — you'll forget wins if you wait
